Part A

Form or use? Error awareness

Week 15 min
Form errorUse error
What it isThe structure is built wrong (wrong ending, wrong auxiliary, missing part).The structure is correct, but it is the wrong choice for the meaning or time.
Example✗ She has wrote three emails. ✓ has written✗ I have visited Boston in 2022. ✓ visited (finished time)

Remember: a mistake is a slip you can fix yourself; an error is something you did not know was wrong. Errors go in your Error Log.

Part B

Present and past tenses

Week 210 min
FormUse it for…Example
Present simplehabits, routines, facts, timetablesShe works in a bank. The store opens at 9.
Present continuousnow, temporary situations, changesShe's working from home this month.
Stative verbsstates, not actions: know, believe, mean, own, belong, need, prefer, seem, like → usually no -ing✗ I'm needing help. ✓ I need help.
Present perfectexperience (ever / never), present result, just, already (+), yet (– / ?)I've already sent the file. Have you ever been to Peru?
Simple pasta finished time: yesterday, last week, in 2021, two days ago, when…I sent the file yesterday.
Two meanings. Some verbs are stative in one meaning and active in another: I think it's a good idea (opinion) vs. I'm thinking about it (process); I have a car vs. I'm having lunch. B2+ be + adjective in the continuous = temporary behavior: The kids are being really quiet. What are they doing?

Part C

Future forms

Week 38 min
FormUse it for…Example
will + basea decision now, a promise, an offer, a prediction from opinionI'll help you. I think it will be easy.
be going to + basea plan already made; a prediction from evidence you can seeI'm going to apply. Watch out, it's going to fall!
Present continuousan arrangement with people, a time, a placeWe're flying to Denver on Monday.
Present simpletimetables and schedulesThe class starts at 2:00.
Future continuous: will be + -ingan action in progress at a future timeAt noon, I'll be driving.
Future perfect: will have + p.p.an action finished before a future time (by…)By May, she'll have saved $2,000.
Register. Will you be + -ing? asks about plans in a soft, polite way: Will you be joining us for lunch? Formal writing often uses the future passive: The results will be posted online.

Part D

Conditionals

Week 410 min
TypeStructureMeaning and example
ZeroIf + present, presentalways true: If you mix blue and yellow, you get green.
FirstIf + present, will + basereal future: If the bus is late, I'll text you.
SecondIf + past, would + baseunreal now / future: If I won the lottery, I'd travel. If I were you, I'd wait.
ThirdIf + had + p.p., would have + p.p.unreal past: If he had checked the map, he wouldn't have gotten lost.
unless = if … not: I'll go unless it's too expensive. as long as / provided (that) = only if: You can stay as long as you're quiet. Common error: no will or would in the if-clause. ✗ If it will snow… ✓ If it snows…

Part E

The passive

Week 512 min

Passive = be + past participle. Change only the form of be for each tense.

Tensebe + past participleExample
Present simpleam / is / are + p.p.Cars are made here.
Present continuousam / is / are being + p.p.The road is being repaired.
Simple pastwas / were + p.p.The letter was sent yesterday.
Past continuouswas / were being + p.p.The car was being washed when it started to rain.
Present perfecthas / have been + p.p.The flight has been delayed.
Future (will)will be + p.p.The winners will be announced soon.
B2+ Future perfectwill have been + p.p.By 2030, the line will have been extended.
Modal passivemodal + be + p.p.The form should be signed in blue ink.

Causative have / get

have / get + object + p.p. = someone does it for you.

I had my car washed.
We need to get the heater fixed.

Why use the passive?

  • the doer is unknown or not important
  • the action or result is the focus
  • formal and academic writing

Add by + doer only if it is useful.

Reporting verbs (formal)

It is said / believed / thought / reported that…
It is thought that the bridge is old.

Subject + is said to + base: He is said to live in Paris.

B2+ past: to have + p.p.: The ship is believed to have sunk in 1850.
